### Pre-incorporation or preliminary contract (Section 17)
>[!note]-
>Co. or promotor can sing contract on behalf of Co. before incorporation of Co. but that contract is not binding to Co. 
>→ accept or,
>→ reject →liability to promotor or Co.


- The right to enter into a contract sues and be sued only after incorporation of the company. Before incorporation companies do not exist. Being nonexistent, it can neither act on its own nor appoint agents. Hence, any contracts entered into in the name of the company before its incorporation becomes *void-ab-initio*, as there are no two parties to the contract, as one party is missing. Hence, a vendor cannot sue or be sued by the company in respect of a pre incorporation contracts after its incorporation. However, section 17 of companies act 2063 provides otherwise.
- **Quasi contract** : When a person lawfully does anything for another person or delivers anything to him without intending to do so, gratuitously and such other person obtains benefit and enjoys it, the latter is bound to compensate the former in respect of, or to restore, the things so done or delivered.
- ==A third person who enters into a pre incorporation contract with the promoter== does so at his own risk, but ==for the benefit of== the proposed ==company,== hence the concept of pre-incorporation ==contract is recognized by the law.==

**meaning (1)**
A contract made prior to the incorporation of a company shall be proposed contract only, and such contracts shall not be binding on the company.

**Liability on the person itself and not company. (2)**
If, prior to the incorporation of a company, any person carries on any transaction or borrows money on behalf of the company, such persons shall be personally liable for any contract related with the transaction, so carried on, subject to the subsection below.

**Liability on the company for pre incorporation contracts (3)**

**Fresh contract in terms of pre-incorporation contract**
<u>Howard V Patent Ivory Manufacturing Co.</u>
If a company, after incorporation, enters into a fresh contract in terms of preliminary incorporation contract the pre-incorporation contractor shall stand canceled, and accordingly the liabilities of promoters shall come to an end

<u>Example 1</u> : Vali Pattabhirama Rao Vs. Sri Ramanuja Ginning and Rice Factory Pvt. Ltd
The promoter of a proposed company entered into a contract with a 3rd party. The contract was entered on behalf of the proposed company. The contract provided that the proposed company shall be lessee of certain properties owned by the 3rd party. [↑]{other party cannot deny contract except company}

The company was incorporated, and afterwards it adopted the lease contract. However, the 3rd party refused to release the properties to the company. It was held that adoption of pre incorporation contract by the company resulted into a binding contract between the company and the 3rd party, and 3rd party was bound by it.

<u>Example 2</u> : The promoters of a proposed company entered into a contract with a 3rd party for allotment of shares of the company after incorporation. It was held that such contracts were not for the purpose of the company and therefore it could not be adopted by the company after incorporation. Therefore such contract was not enforceable by the company or the 3rd party.

### Share capital and its categories
A the total capital of the company is divided into shares, the capital of the company is called "share capital"

**Authorized share capital**
- The amount of capital mentioned in the capital class of Memorandum of Association Register with the registrar of companies is the authorized their capital of the company and is also known as registered capital or nominal capital
- It is an estimation of maximum capital requirement
- It puts a limit on the amount of capital, which a company is authorized to raise during its lifetime and is called "Authorized capital"
- It is shown in the Balance sheet at face value
- Authorize capital shops three major purpose:
	1. It shows the investment for financial strength or creditworthiness of a company as the money raised by issuing shares to the extent of authorized capital
	2. The revenue or fee of registration of a company is fixed as per the authorized share capital
	3. It increases the borrowing capacity of the company

**Issued share capital**
- Whatever portion of the share capital is issued by the company is called issued share capital i.e. it means and includes the nominal value of shares issued by the company for cash or consideration other than cash
- It is shown in the balance sheet at face value
- Un-issued share capital = authorize share capital - issued share capital
- Un-issued share capital is not shown in balance sheet

**Subscribed share capital**
The part of share capital, which is subscribed by the public i.e. applied and allotted by the company is subscribed share capital

**Called-up share capital**
The portion of the issue price of shares which a company has demanded or called from shareholder is known as called-up share capital and the balance portion is uncalled share capital

**Paid-up share capital**
- It is a portion of called up capital which is paid by the shareholders
- Whenever a particular amount is called by the company and the shareholder fails to pay the amount fully or partially, it is known as unpaid calls or installments(calls) in arrears

**Reserve share capital**
Portion of uncalled capital which a company has decided to call only in case of liquidation of the company is called reserve capital

### Procedure for issue of shares

**Private companies**
Private companies issue shares in "Private Placement"

**Public companies: Initial Public offering and Follow-on/Further Public offering**
1. Step 1: Issue a prospectus and invite general public to subscribe for shares.
2. Step 2: on the basis of prospectus applications are deposited in a bank by the interested parties along with the amount payable at the time of application in cash. The first installment paid along with the application is called application money
3. Step 3: Minimum subscription
4. Step 4: Allotment of shares to public
5. Step 5: Collection of issue price in installments known as call money (first call , second call, and so on till final call).

**Issue of shares**
- At par i.e. at face value
- At premium i.e. at more than face value (section 29)
- At discount i.e. at less than face value (section 64)


### Shares issued in lieu of service contract (companies directives 2072)
Sears issued by any company to any person from whom it had received some service are called shares issued in lieu of service contract.
Example: a company issued share of the amount of agreement between the company and an engineer who had provided service of designing and drawing the structure of building to be constructed

If any shares are to be issued in lieu of service contract or written agreement clearly specifying the remuneration of service and number of shares that may be issued in lieu of remuneration must be made between two parties
