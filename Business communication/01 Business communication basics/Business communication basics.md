## Succeeding in Work Places 

- **The success of an organization is significantly influenced by the status and skills of its human resources(individuals within the organization).**
- The success of an organization is typically measured by the achievements and positive outcomes it attains as a collective entity, rather than focusing solely on the accomplishments of individuals within the organization.
- An organization is a separate entity that has a common goal whereas individuals within the organization are guided by various factors such as their skills, motivation, and leadership.
**Every activities and performances of employees are mostly guided by two fundamental drives: Ascribed traits and achieved traits.**
- <u><strong>Ascribed Traits</strong></u>: Ascribed traits are characteristics or attributes that individuals inherit or are assigned involuntarily. These traits are often beyond an individual's control and are not earned through personal effort.
- **Examples:** Gender, ethnicity, age, and certain aspects of one's background or upbringing can be considered ascribed traits.
- **Impact on Work:** Ascribed traits can influence how individuals are perceived by others in the workplace. Unfortunately, biases or stereotypes based on these traits may affect opportunities or interactions.

---



**Achieved Traits:** Achieved traits are characteristics or attributes that individuals acquire through their efforts, skills, education, and accomplishments. These traits are earned and developed over time.
• **Examples:** Education, job skills, professional certifications, and work experience are examples of achieved traits.
• **Impact on Work:** Achieved traits are often directly related to an individual's performance and success in the workplace. They can contribute to career advancement, job effectiveness, and overall professional growth.

• Organizations that recognize and value both types of traits contribute to a more inclusive and merit-based workplace.

➢ **Promoting Fairness**

➢ **Diversity and Inclusion**

➢ **Training and Education**

➢ **Merit-Based Evaluations:** Encourage managers to assess employees based on their performance and merit rather than relying on assumptions tied to ascribed traits.

➢ **Equal Opportunities**

➢ **Mentorship and Sponsorship Programs**

➢ **Feedback and Recognition**

## Changing Trend of Management Structure 

- Several notable trends have emerged in recent years, reflecting a shift in how organizations structure their management. Some key factors behind the changing trends in management structure:

1. **Globalization of global market economy**
	- **Globalization of the global market economy refers to the interconnectedness and interdependence of economies around the world.** It means that countries are connected and depend on each other. Goods, services, money, information, and people move more and more between countries.

2. **Flatter Hierarchies and team based management:**
	- Traditional hierarchical structures are giving way to flatter organizational hierarchies. This involves reducing the number of hierarchical levels, promoting quicker decision-making, and fostering a more responsive organization.
	- Organizations are increasingly moving towards team-based structures where cross-functional teams collaborate to achieve specific goals. This promotes a more collaborative and flexible approach to work.

---

3. **Technological advancement**: The adoption of collaboration tools and technologies has become integral to modern management structures. These tools facilitate communication, information sharing, and collaboration among team members, even in virtual environments.

4. **Multicultural workforce:** employees come from diverse cultural backgrounds. Having a multicultural workforce is often seen as a strength because it can bring a range of perspectives and ideas, enriching the workplace environment. It can also contribute to better problem-solving, creativity, and innovation.

5. **Focus on Employee Empowerment and Employee Well-being:**
	- Organizations are recognizing the importance of empowering employees by providing them with the autonomy to make decisions and contribute to the decision-making process.
	- Organizations are recognizing the importance of creating environments that support work-life balance, mental health, and overall employee satisfaction.

### Barriers in the Communication Process 

- Barriers to communication are things that get in the way of a message being received. They could be physical, such as loud music playing, or emotional, such as when a person is too angry or fearful to listen to what another individual is saying. Culture, language, and social status can also represent barriers to effective communication. Communicators need to be aware of barriers and how to overcome them to improve the communication process.

- <span style="color:rgb(146, 208, 80)">Lack of language skill</span>: Those with insufficient language skills may struggle to convey their thoughts accurately, leading to incomplete or unclear communication.
- <span style="color:rgb(146, 208, 80)">vocabulary:</span> if you choose the wrong words in your message, it creates barrier.
- <span style="color:rgb(146, 208, 80)">Jargon:</span> Jargon is a technical word of a particular fields. Jargon shouldn't be used when communication is between two or more people that aren't familiar with the specific terminology.
- <span style="color:rgb(146, 208, 80)">Frame of reference:</span> A frame of reference in communication refers to the perspective, experiences, values, and cultural background that individuals bring to an interaction. Differences in frames of reference can lead to communication barriers. For example: For one person can have adirect eye contact while speaking to show attentiveness and sincerity while another can avoid direct eye contact while listening to show attentiveness and respect.
- <span style="color:rgb(146, 208, 80)">Bypassing</span> : the true meaning of a message is not directly addressed or acknowledged.
- <span style="color:rgb(146, 208, 80)">Slang</span>: informal words or phrases that are used in day to day conversations and are often taken negatively in professional setting. Eg: “yaar”
- <span style="color:rgb(146, 208, 80)">Abstract and ambiguous words:</span> Business communication should be precise, simple and concise. Confusing or vague words can create confusion. For example: as soon as possible
- <span style="color:rgb(146, 208, 80)">Wrong Translation:</span> while translating, you must understand the other person’s cultural background etc.
- <span style="color:rgb(146, 208, 80)">Noise and distraction:</span> Noise is the biggest barriers that prevents from sending and receiving the message.
- <span style="color:rgb(146, 208, 80)">Filters:</span> For example in Twitter you have a word limit to post a status. Which can prevent you from expressing your message.
- <span style="color:rgb(146, 208, 80)">Channel breakdown</span>: Faulty equipment can create problems in the communication
- <span style="color:rgb(146, 208, 80)">A cliché</span>: A cliché is a word or phrase that has been overused and therefore lost its effectiveness.

- <span style="color:rgb(146, 208, 80)">Semantic barrier</span>: The meaning of words, signs and symbols might be different from one person to another and the same word might have hundreds of meanings. **Semantic barriers to communication are the symbolic obstacles that distorts the sent message in some other way than intended, making the message difficult to understand.** It also arises due to language, education, culture and place of origin or experiences.
	- i) **Denotative Barriers**: Direct meaning of any word which must be shared by two people to understand each other is the denotative meaning. The barriers that arise due to the definition or meaning of a word used differently by sender and receiver is denotative barriers of communication. For example, the meaning of braces which is used to define the metallic structure to adjust teeth in American English whereas it means a part of clothing in British English.
	- ii) **Connotative barrier:** Connotative barrier in communication refers to the difference of meaning according to different abstract situations, contexts, actions and feelings. For example, the word crazy can be used to describe as having an abnormal mind as well as being very excited or pleased.

1. **The false necessity trap**: occurs when a company thinks it has to do something, even if it's ethically questionable, because it believes it's the only way to succeed.
2. **Doctrine-of-relative filth trap**: some unethical actions may look harmless compared to others.

4. **Self-deception trap** : e.g. interviewee exaggerates past work successes or uni grades.

### Major Perspectives of ethics

- Economical Perspective: Looking at ethics from an economic perspective means thinking about how doing the right thing in business can affect how well the business does financially. It's like understanding that being ethical is not just about being a good person; it can also make a business more successful. When businesses make ethical choices, it can lead to positive results in the short term (immediate) and the long term (over a longer period). - Philosophical Perspective: involves looking at what is morally right and wrong in business practices. It's about thinking deeply about the principles and values that guide decisions and actions in the business world. In simple words, it means considering the bigger questions about what is fair, just, and morally acceptable in how businesses operate. This perspective encourages businesses to think beyond just following rules and laws and to reflect on the ethical principles that should guide their behavior.

# Unethical communication 

- Unethical communication in business refers to practices or behaviors that violate ethical principles, standards, or norms in the realm of business communication. Unethical communication can have various forms and negative consequences, impacting relationships with stakeholders, damaging a company's reputation, and even leading to legal issues.
- **Misrepresentation:** Providing false or misleading information about products, services, or the company's financial status can be considered unethical.
- **Deceptive Advertising**: Making exaggerated or false claims in advertising to manipulate consumers or create a false perception about a product or service is unethical.
- **Plagiarism and Intellectual Property Violations:** Copying someone else's work, ideas, or intellectual property without proper attribution is unethical.
- **Insider Trading:** Sharing confidential information about a company for personal gain or using privileged information to make financial decisions is unethical.
- **Discriminatory Communication:** Discriminating against individuals based on factors such as race, gender, age, or ethnicity in communication practices, such as hiring, promotions, or client interactions, is unethical.
- **Bullying and Harassment:** Engaging in bullying, harassment, or creating a hostile work environment through communication is unethical.
- **Selective Disclosure:** Providing information selectively to favor certain stakeholders over others is unethical.
- **Conflict of Interest:** Failing to disclose personal or financial interests that may influence business decisions can be considered unethical.
- **Whistleblower Retaliation:** Punishing or retaliating against employees who report unethical behavior within the organization is itself an unethical practice.
- **Lack of Transparency:** Withholding important information from stakeholders that could impact their decisions or well-being is unethical.
- **Data Privacy Violations:** Mishandling or improperly using personal and sensitive data, such as customer information, violates privacy standards and is unethical.
