A reporting entity is the specific unit for which financial statements are prepared. It's not necessarily a legal entity, but rather a defined area of activity for which financial information is required, potentially encompassing multiple legal entities. Reporting entities are typically required by law to adhere to accounting standards when creating their financial reports, often referred to as "general purpose financial reports". 
Here's a more detailed breakdown:
**Not a Legal Entity:**
A reporting entity can include parts of a legal entity, an entire legal entity, or even a combination of legal entities. 
**Purpose:**
The primary purpose of a reporting entity is to provide a specific set of financial information for users who rely on it. 
**Examples:**
A sole proprietorship, a branch of a company, a corporation, or even a group of related companies under a common control can all be reporting entities. 
**Financial Reporting:**
Reporting entities are responsible for preparing and presenting financial statements that comply with applicable accounting standards. 
**Legal Obligations:**
In many jurisdictions, reporting entities have legal obligations to report their financial information, such as filing annual reports with regulatory bodies.
