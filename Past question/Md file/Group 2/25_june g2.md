### Question no. 6c (FM)

 c) Spin Off 

A spin-off in financial management is a strategic corporate action where a company creates a new, independent entity by separating a portion of its existing operations, assets, or divisions. This process typically involves distributing shares of the newly formed entity to the parent company's existing shareholders on a pro-rata basis. The spun-off company operates as a distinct legal entity, with its own management team, operations, and financial structure, allowing it to pursue its own growth strategy and market opportunities. Spin-offs are often pursued when a parent company believes that separating certain business units will enhance value for shareholders by allowing each entity to focus on its core operations.
From a financial perspective, a spin-off can create value by improving operational efficiency, enhancing management focus, and increasing shareholder value. The spun-off company can pursue its own growth strategies, often unburdened by the parent company's broader corporate structure. For the parent company, a spin-off can lead to a more streamlined business, potentially improving profitability and market valuation. Additionally, spin-offs can help unlock value that might have been hidden within a larger, diversified organization. Investors may appreciate the distinct market focus of each entity, and the separation allows both companies to be valued independently by the market.

### Question no. 3c (Cost and Management Accounting)

c) Differentiate between Cost control and Cost Reduction.

 Answer

|   | Cost Control | Cost Reduction  |
|  |  |  |
|  1 | It represents efforts made towards achieving a target or goal. | It represents achievements of real and permanent reduction in cost  |
|  2 | The process of cost control is to set up a target, investigate the variances variations and taking remedial measures to correct them. | Cost reduction is not contended merely with maintenance of performance according to the standards  |
|  3 | It assumes existence of standards or norms which are not challenged. | It assumes existence concealed potential savings in the standards or norms which are therefore subject to constant challenge or improvement.  |
|  4 | It is preventive function. Costs are optimized they are incurred. | It is corrective function. It operates even when efficient cost control system exists. There is room for reduction in the achieved costs.  |
|  5 | It lacks dynamic approach. | It takes a dynamic approach to reduce cost without impairing the quality and suitability of the quality of product  |
|  6 | Budgetary control and standard costing are essential tools and techniques of cost control. | Cost reduction uses tools and techniques such as job evaluation, merit rewards, standardization, simplification, incentives, improvement in design, automation, etc.  |

### Question no. 5b (Cost and Management Accounting)

b. What do you mean by inter firm comparison? What are its limitations?

 Answer:5(b) 

It is a technique of evaluating the performance, efficiency, costs and profits of firms in an industry. It consists of voluntary exchange of information/data concerning costs, prices, profits, productivity and overall efficiency among firms engaged in similar types of operations for the purpose of bringing improvement in efficiency and indicating weaknesses. Such a comparison is possible where uniform costing is in operation.

The following are the limitations in the implementation of inter firm comparison.

1. Business firms may be afraid that disclosure of information may help competitors and may thus be reluctant to part with facts which are detrimental to their interests.
2. Some managements are not convinced about the utility of inter-firm comparison.
3. If business firms have not established suitable cost accounting systems, the information supplied by them may not be usable for inter-firm comparison.
4. Business firms usually do not agree about a common suitable basis for comparison.

However, the above limitations are basically due to differences in the nature of participating business firms and use of accounting methods. Therefore, a uniform cost accounting system should be established in every participating firm and business firms should be educated about the advantages and uses of inter-firm comparison.

### Question no. 2 (Marketing)

6. What is pricing? Explain the factors affecting price determination.

 Answer

Price is the exchange value that is used to purchase the goods and services. Price is the amount of money and it is sacrificed to obtain the goods and services. Price is the one of the primary elements of marketing mix that the marketing manager must consider while preparing the marketing programs. Price is the amount of money and other items with utility needed to acquire a goods and services. Pricing is the function of determining the value of product in monetary term. Pricing is the process of determination appropriate value of particular goods and services.
There are several factors which influence to the determination of price. They can be internal and external factors, which are as follows;

 a) Internal factors

Internal factors are those factors which are located inside the organization. They can be controlled by the organization to a large extent. Therefore, it is also called the controllable variables. The internal factors which influence in the determination price are as follows;
i) Pricing objectives: The pricing objectives of the organization directly influence to the determination of price of goods and services. If organization has the profit maximization objectives, the price of the product can be high and if organization has the sales maximization pricing objectives the price become lower. If the organization has the competitive pricing objective price can be determined as the price of competitors.
ii) Cost: Every price of the product must cover at least costs involved in the production, distribution, promotion, and other support of marketing activities. Generally, the price of the product is set based on the costs incurred for production, promotion, distribution and other administrative cost by adding the required profit. Therefore, the cost is the main factor which influence in the determination of price.



iii)Other element of marketing mix: Product, distribution and promotion are the other elements of marketing mix which influence in the setting the price which are as follows;
$>$ Product: Basically, the types and nature of product influence in the determination of price. High price is set for the innovative product, copy products are sold under the competitive price, slightly high price is set for modified products, luxurious products demands the high price and necessity goods demands the lower price.
$>$ Place: Price is based on the use of distribution channel. Price can be high in the long distribution channel and lower in the short distribution channel. Price is determined by the profit taken by distribution channel and commission given to the distribution channel. The modes of transport for distribution also influence to the price of the product. Expensive modes of transport like air transport is expensive and the price become high and land transport, water transport are the low cost of the transport and the price become lower.
$>$ Promotion: Promotion methods and its tools influence in the setting of the price. The price is high if aggressive advertisement and sales promotion is used for the product and the price will be lower, if organization has used publicity. The promotional media used for promotion also determine the price of the product. If organization has used the expensive promotional media the price will be high and if media has low cost then the price will be low.
iv) Business efficiency: Major internal factor which influence in the price determination is business efficiency of the marketing firm. When the marketing firm has high business efficiency for marketing activities such as purchasing of raw material, production process, material handling, it results in the reduction of cost and it influence in the lowering the price and if there is low business efficiency, it the price will be high.

 b) External factors 

External factors are those forces which are located outside the organization. They cannot be controlled by the organization to a large extent. Therefore, they are called uncontrollable variables which are as follows;
i) Market demand: The market demand is the important factor which influence to the price determination. When the demand of the goods and services are high, the consumers are ready to pay more prices then the price of the product will be high and when the demand of the product is low then the price of the product will be low. So, marketer must understand the demand of the product of goods and services in the setting the price.
ii) Competition: Marketer cannot ignore the competitors while setting the price of goods and services. The competitor's action regarding the price influence in the determination of price because organization should determine the price based on the competitors. If there is high competition the price will be lower and if there is no competition the price can be high.
iii) Government: Government policies, rules and regulations affects in the determination of price. The tax imposed to the organization also influence to the price determination. Price is low if government provides subsidy. In Nepalese context, government determines the price of some products such as sugar, oil, electricity, transportation, salary, telephone, and other public utilities. Organization is not free to set the price of these products.
iv) Marketing intermediaries: There are different types of marketing intermediaries for the distribution of goods. If the marketing intermediaries charges high profit margin and commission in the distribution of goods, the price will be high and if marketing intermediaries charges the low profit margin and commission, the price will be lower.
v) Pressure groups: In every country, there exist the pressure groups, and pressure groups put the pressure to the business organization to set the price according to their interest. They put the pressure to the government to control the price of goods and services and to maintain the quality of goods and services.
vi) Economic condition: There are different types of economic conditions in the market. In the prosperity economic condition, the purchasing power of customers is high and the price of the goods and services will be high. In the recession economic condition, the purchasing power might be low and the price of the goods and services will be lower. In the inflation period, the price of the product will high.

 Answer

Market segmentation is the process of dividing a broad market into smaller, clearly defined groups of consumers who have similar needs, characteristics, or behaviors. Each segment can then be targeted with specific marketing strategies and products that match their unique preferences. The main goal of segmentation is to better satisfy customer needs and improve marketing effectiveness.

 Requirements for Effective Market Segmentation:

 1. Measurable:

The size, purchasing power, and characteristics of the segments must be quantifiable.

 2. Accessible:

The segments should be reachable and serviceable through existing marketing and distribution channels.

 3. Substantial:

The segment must be large and profitable enough to serve. Small segments may not justify the investment. The market segment should be large enough to give the organization adequate profit.

 4. Differentiable:

Segmentation of a market is meaningful only when significant difference exists in buyers' product needs. The segments should respond differently to different marketing strategies or product offerings.

 5. Actionable:

The business must be able to design effective programs to attract and serve the segments.
