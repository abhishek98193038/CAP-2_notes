### Question no. 7d (FM)

Distinguish between:

d) Nominal interest rate and effective interest rate

**Q. 7 d. Answer:**

The interest rate that is specified on an annual basis in a loan agreement or security (say, in the case of a bond) is known as the nominal interest rate. Thus, the nominal interest rate is the simple interest rate (without compounding) which is stated in the face of a security or a loan agreement. Usually, there are provisions of compounding of the interest payable on a loan or security. The compounding may be done monthly, quarterly or semi-annually. When compounding of the interest payable is done more than once a year, the actual annualized interest would be higher than the nominal interest rate and it is called the effective interest rate.

The general formula for calculating effective interest rate (EIR) can be written in the following form:
$\mathrm{EIR}=[1+\mathrm{i} / \mathrm{m}]^{\mathrm{nx} \mathrm{m}}-1$, where
i denotes the annual nominal rate of interest,
n denotes the number of year and
m denotes the number of compounding per year.
For instance, in the case of annual compounding, $\mathrm{m}=1$, in quarterly compounding m $=4$, and in the case of monthly compounding, $\mathrm{m}=12$. The formula given above can be modified to accomplish the multi-period compounding for any number of years.

4. a) Mr. Shivapati is an Indian national living in his own hometown. He is an arbitration specialist and provides service to offshore companies on dispute settlement. He was contacted by Nepal Government and asked to join arbitration team of Nepal on international dispute handling. He was interested in the offer and intended to join the team. The government hired him under employment contract and was stationed in Nepali Embassy of India in FY 2079/80. Is Mr. Shivapati considered resident for Income Tax purpose and where is the source of the payment considered? Also mention applicable tax rate.


 Answer 

4 a) As per Income Tax Act, 2058, section 2(ka nga) Natural Person is resident if any of following conditions are met,
a. If normal place of abode is in Nepal,
b. If $\mathrm{s} /$ he stays in Nepal for 183 days or more during any period of consecutive 365 days, or
c. If $\mathrm{s} /$ he is deputed by Government of Nepal to a foreign country in any time of the income year.

In given case, Mr. Shivapati is an Indian national, an arbitration expert, hired by Nepal Government on employment contract. He is then deputed to Nepali Embassy in India as per the terms of employment contract. Mr. Shivapati though is permanent resident of India, is hired by the government of Nepal and deputed in Nepali Embassy. Although the conditions of normal place of abode and minimum 183 days stay in Nepal are not met, the third condition, when an individual deputed by Government of Nepal to a foreign country, is satisfied, therefore he is resident for the income year 2079/80.

As per Income Tax Act, 2058, section 67(6)(jha), Payments in the following circumstances inclusive of service charges, other than mentioned in clauses (g) or (h) of the section 67(6), for servicing employment or rendering service or accepting restriction in those acts is deemed as payment source in Nepal.
(1) Where the acts are carried out in Nepal irrespective of the place of payment, or

i) In the case of body corporate, if the body corporate is closed down, sold or transferred or if the body corporate in any manner ceases to exist;
ii) In the case of an individual ownership, if the owner dies;
iii) In the case of a partnership firm, if it is dissolved or the partner dies;
iv) If a registered person ceases to be engaged in taxable transactions;
v) If the taxpayer files for zero return or does not file return at all within a consecutive period of twelve months,
vi) If the taxable transaction of the taxpayer does not reach fifty lakhs in case of goods, and twenty lakhs in case of services and transaction mixed with goods and services in past twelve months,
vii) if registered mistakenly.

Taxpayer shall produce his or her documents for auditing within 15 days of the filing of the tax returns for cancellation of registration; and the Tax Officer shall perform the audit of such a taxpayer, and inform the taxpayer of cancellation of registration or if the registration is not to be so cancelled, the Tax Officer shall give information thereof to the taxpayer. If the Tax Officer does not cancel registration or make any decision to cancel registration within that period, the taxpayer is not obliged to file the tax returns subsequent to that period.

Out of the goods that have already enjoyed the tax credit facility, the stock including capital goods as well remaining at the time of the cancellation of registration shall be deemed to have been supplied at the market value and tax shall be assessed and recovered accordingly.
