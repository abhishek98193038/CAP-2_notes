### Question no. 1 (FM)
1. Auriga (Healthcare) has invested Rs. 220,000 over the past two years in the development of a personal stress monitoring device (PSMD). The device is designed for busy individuals wishing to check their stress levels. Market research that was commissioned earlier in the year at a cost of Rs. 45,000 suggests that the price for the PSMD should be Rs. 22 per unit and that the expected product life cycle of the device is four years.

In order to produce the device, the business must purchase immediately specialist machinery and equipment at a cost of Rs. 300,000. This machinery and equipment has an expected life of four years and will have no residual value at the end of this period. The machinery and equipment can produce a maximum of 15,000 PSMDs per year over four years. To ensure that the maximum output is achieved, the business will spend Rs. 50,000 a year in advertising the device over the next four years.

It is a material that is currently used in other areas of the business and the cost of replacing the polymer is Rs. 1.50 per 100 grams. ### Question no. 5c (FM)

**Q.5c Answer:**

According to Walter Model, Market Price per share $=\frac{D P S+\left(\frac{r}{R_{p}}\right)(E P S-D P S)}{K_{p}}$

**Q.6d Answer:**

 Q.7a Answer: 

Assets of a leveraged firm are financed by debt and equity. Therefore, the assets beta should be the weighted average of the equity beta and the debt beta. There is also a linear relationship between the equity beta and the financial leverage. As the financial leverage increases, the equity beta also increases. The equity beta is equal to the asset beta if debt is zero.

### Question no. 7d (FM)

 d) Factoring and Bill Discounting 

 Q.7d Answer:

The main differences between Factoring and Bill discounting are:
(1) While factoring is management of book-debts, bill discounting is a sort of borrowing from commercial banks.
(2) In factoring no grace period is given, whereas in bill discounting grace period is 3 days.
(3) For factoring there is no Specific Act, whereas in case of bill discounting, Negotiable Instruments Act applies.
(4) Factoring is a portfolio of complementary financial services whereas bill discounting is usually on case to case basis.
(5) In factoring the basis of financing is turnover. Whereas in bill discounting it is the security provision as well as the requirement of finance which determine the amount of financing.
(6) In factoring the risk of bad debts is passed on to the factor, whereas in bill discounting it is still retained by the business.

### Question no. 6b (Cost and Management Accounting)

b) Criticisms of marginal costing.

**Q.6b Answer:**

i. Planning and controlling of cost problem: Marginal costing places too much emphasis on direct costs and pays cursory attention to the fixed production overhead costs which may not be of help to the managers in planning and controlling of fixed production overhead costs from the beginning of the production process. In reality, all costs must be planned and controlled.
ii. Problem of application in an automated production environment: marginal costing method is not appropriate in an automated production system where fixed production overhead costs are far higher than the direct costs. Since the method does not consider the fixed production overhead cost at the beginning, when gathering the costs of production, important costs may be considered too late.
iii. Confusion on treatment of overhead cost: The management team may be under the illusion that the marginal costing method completely ignores fixed production overhead costs, which may lead to confusion and argument later when calculating the profit or loss at the end of the period.
iv. Price quotation problem: The marginal costing method may not be suitable for price quotation of jobs or contracts that involve huge overhead cost, since the overhead costs are ignored at the early stage of the calculation of the total costs

 c) Differences between formal and informal communication

 Q. 4c Answer:

Formal communication is structured, follows official channels, and often involves documentation, making it appropriate for professional settings where accuracy and protocol are essential. It includes emails, reports, and meetings with an established agenda. Informal communication, on the other hand, is more casual and spontaneous, often taking place in personal conversations, social media, or instant messaging. While formal communication is typically used for official decisions and information sharing, informal communication is more relaxed, facilitating quicker interactions and often fostering better rapport among colleagues.


 d) Techniques of conflict management 

 Q. 4d Answer:

- Mediation: In this technique, disputes are facilitated by third party intervention. The third party plays the role of mediator that takes initiatives for negotiation and agreement between the two conflicting parties.
- Negotiation: This technique is based on win-win theory of conflict management. Negotiation takes place through the process of intra-group facilitation. The conflicting parties have mutual compromise and co-ordination for the negotiation between them.
- Arbitration: This is a legal attempt to manage the conflict. It is rather technical and rigid. Disputes are legally analyzed. Forceful impositions can also be used to settle the conflict.
- Reconciliation: This technique is an institutional practice to bring the conflicting groups together into a consensus. It looks for the strategic settlement of the opposite groups. An attempt is made to look for interdependence and co-existence of the conflicting parties.

 a) What is marketing research? Explain the process of marketing research. 

 Q.3a Answer:

When the marketing manager face some types of marketing problems then marketing manager must conduct the marketing research to solve of marketing problems. Marketing research refers to the systematic design, collection, analysis and reporting of data relevant to specific marketing situation facing by company. Marketing research is the application of scientific principles of observational, experimental, historical and survey methods in a careful search for more accurate knowledge of consumers and market behavior so that more effective marketing and distribution may be developed. Followings are the marketing research process;

1. Define the problem and state the research objectives: First of all marketing researcher should identify the real problem that is facing by the company and all the research works must be concentrated towards solving those identified problems. Likewise, in this stage researcher should define the research objective. The research objective must be based on the identified research problem and all the research works are guided by the research objective.
2. Developing the research plan: Secondly, researcher should develop the research plan to address the research questions and research objectives. Research plan refers to the future course of action regarding the research work. Research plan consists of determination of types of data, determining the methods for collection of data, determining the instruments for collection of data, defining the population of study, determining the sampling methods, determining the sample size of the study, and mentioning the methods for analyzing the collected data.
3. Data collection: In the third stage of marketing research, researcher should collect the data as defined in the research plan. Data can be primary and secondary. To collect the secondary data is easy because they can be collected by visiting the institutions or websites. But to collect the primary data is very difficult and time consuming. Questionnaire survey, observation, focus discussion and interview can be used for the collection of primary data.
4. Data presentation and analysis: In the fourth stage, researcher should present and analyze the collected data. Data analysis consists of processing of data, tabulation of data, presentation of data in table and analyzing through analytical tools. There are different methods of analyzing the data. They can be descriptive analysis and inferential analysis. Mean, standard deviation, percentage, mode, median etc. can be used as descriptive analysis and correlation, regression etc. can be used as inferential statistics to analyze the data. Based on the results data can analyzed as research objectives.
5. Preparation of research report: At the last stage, researcher mentions the findings of the study prepare the conclusions of the study and prepare the report of the study to submit to the concerned parties. If everything is all right, researcher should prepare the final draft for the final report by drawing suitable conclusions, and then present the report to the marketing manager or concerned authorities.

**Q. 5 Answer:**
 b. Self- Assessment

Every person except those persons who are exempt from filing income tax return shall assess their taxable income and file income tax return accordingly. This is called self- Tax Assessment. There are two types of self-assessment.

- Actual Self-assessment: When the person files a return disclosing the income tax payable by the person for the year, Self-tax assessment is treated to be made on due date for filing income tax return.
- Deemed Self-Assessment: When the person fails to file income tax return for any income year on due date, then until such return is filed, self-tax assessment is deemed to be made on due date of filing return. In such case, Income tax payable by the person is deemed to sum of any withholding tax deposited in name of person and advance tax paid by the person and there is no income tax Payable.

a) Proportionate Credit

**Q. 8 Answer:**

a) Proportionate Credit

If registered person deals with both VAT attractive and VAT exempt items, VAT credit is allowed on proportionate basis in case of VAT input relating to common costs.
As per rule 40 (3), VAT paid on purchases directly attributable to VAT attractive output is fully allowed for VAT credit. Similarly, VAT paid on purchases directly attributable to VAT exempted output is not allowed for VAT credit.
As per rule 40 (4) ,VAT paid on common costs, which cannot be directly attributable to VAT attractive or VAT exempt output, is allowed for VAT credit on the proportion of VAT attractive and VAT exempt sales.
