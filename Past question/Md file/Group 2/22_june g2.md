### Question no 1 (Financial Management)

1. F1 Soft Security Systems Ltd, manufactures devices which seek to deter the theft of motor vehicles. The company's development department has recently produced a new type of anti-theft device. This device, which will be known as an "Vehicle Protector", will be fitted to private motor vehicles. The device emits an electronic signal which can be picked up by an electronic sensor fitted to police cars, enabling the police to locate stolen cars and, possibly, apprehend the thief. Development costs totalled Rs. 500,000. These were all incurred in 2078.

A decision now needs to be taken as to whether to go ahead with producing and marketing Vehicle Protector. This is to be based on the expected net present value of the relevant cash flows, discounted at the company's estimate of the 2078 weighted average cost of capital of $8 \%$ (after tax). The company's management believes that a three-year planning horizon is appropriate for this decision, so it will be assumed that sales will not continue beyond 2081.

Following discussions with a number of police forces, the company has reached agreement that, if it decides to go ahead with the project, Kathmandu Valley forces will trial the Vehicle Protector system, and the sensors will be fitted to its police cars.

The cost of providing and fitting the sensors to the police cars would be borne by the company, which would retain ownership. The police force would bear the cost of maintenance. The sensors would be manufactured and fitted by a sub-contractor, who has offered to do this work by the end of 2078 for a total cost of Rs. 1 million, payable immediately on completion of the work. This cost would attract the $25 \%$ (WDV) tax depreciation for plant and equipment since from the year of purchase. At the end of three years the sensors would be scrapped at zero value and remaining written down value (WDV) to be treated as balancing allowance.

The first sales of device would be expected to be made during the year ending 2079. There is uncertainty as to the level of sales which could be expected, so a market survey has been undertaken at a cost of Rs. 100,000.

The survey suggests that, at the company's target ex-works price of Rs. 200 per device, there would be a $60 \%$ chance of selling 10,000 Vehicle Protector and a $40 \%$ chance of selling 12,000 during 2079. If the 2079 sales were to be at the lower level, 2080 sales would be either 8,000 Vehicle Protector ( $30 \%$ chance) or 10,000 ( $70 \%$ chance). If 2079 sales were to be at the higher level, 2080 sales would be estimated at 12,000 Vehicle Protector ( $50 \%$ chance) or 15,000 ( $50 \%$ chance). 2081 sales would be expected to be $50 \%$ of whatever level of sales were to occur in 2080.

Sales of Vehicle Protector would be expected to have an adverse effect on sales of the old Vehicle Tracker Device, a less sophisticated device produced by the company, to the extent that for every two Vehicle Protector sold one less Vehicle Tracker Device would be sold. This effect would be expected to continue throughout the three years.

Materials and components would be bought in at a cost of Rs. 70 per Vehicle Protector. Manufacture of each Vehicle Protector would require three hours of labor. This labor would come from staff released by the lost Vehicle Tracker Device production. To the extent that this would provide insufficient hours, staff would work overtime, paid at a premium of $50 \%$ over the basic pay of Rs. 6 an hour.

**Answer:** 

a)

Book building is a technique used for marketing a public offer of equity shares of a company. It is a way of raising more funds from the market.

A company can use the process of book building to fine tune its price of issue. When a company employs book building mechanism, it does not pre-determine the issue price (in case of equity shares) or interest rate (in case of debentures) and invite subscription to the issue. Instead, it starts with an indicative price band (or interest band) which is determined through consultative process with its merchant banker and asks its merchant banker to invite bids from prospective investors at different prices (or different rates). Those who bid for the process are required to pay the full amount. Based on the response received from investors the final price is selected. The merchant banker (called in this case Book Runner) has to manage the entire book building process. Investors who have bid a price equal to or more than the final price selected are given allotment at the final price selected. Those who have bid for a lower price will get their money refunded.

The greatest advantage of the book building process is that this allows for price and demand discovery. Secondly, the cost of issue is much less than the other traditional methods of raising capital. In book building, the demand for shares is known before the issue closes. In fact, if there is not much demand the issue may be deferred and can be rescheduled after having realised the temper of the market.

### Question no 6b (Financial Management)

b) Pecking order theory






**Answer:** 
b)

The pecking order theory of corporate capital structure developed by states that issuing securities is subject to an adverse selection problem. Managers endowed with private information have incentives to issue overpriced risky securities. But they also understand that issuing such securities will result in a negative price reaction because rational investors, who are at an information disadvantage, will discount the prices of any risky securities the firm issues. Consequently, firms follow a pecking order: use internal resources when possible; if internal funds are inadequate, obtain external debt; external equity is the last resort.
Firms simply use all their internally generated funds first then move down the pecking order to debt and then finally to issuing new equity, firms follow a line of least resistance that establishes the capital structure.

Investment decisions are affected by the risk attitude or risk behaviour of investor making the decisions normally risk averse and risk seeker investor. Market segmentation is a marketing strategy that involves dividing a broad target market into a subset of consumers who have common needs or desires and using media channels and other touchpoints that best allow reaching them. Market segmentation is also known as market segregation. It is the process of dividing the heterogeneous market into a homogeneous market. It is the process, by which a marketer attempts to match the needs and wants of customers having similar needs and behaviors. Market segmentation is a division of a total market into groups. Market Segmentation is achieved on a four-stage process:

 Step 1: Segment Identification

The first step in the segmentation process is to identify the market segments for the product. The market segment is identified by the survey, analysis, and profiling. The survey is conducted to collect the data related to the need of the market and to study the characteristics of the market. In a survey, demographic features, psychological, behavioral, and geographical features are studied. - Segment size and growth
- Segment structural attractiveness
- Organizational objective and resources

**Answer:**
