His monthly remuneration in USA is equivalent to Nepalse Rs 5,00,000 per month. Determine the residential status and taxability of income of Mr. Shyam Khadka in Nepal.
ii) Mr. Ram Bansal, an India citizen, has joined a Cement Industry in Nepal as a Chief- Engineer Maintenance on $1^{\text {st }}$ Mangsir 2072 at a monthly gross remuneration of Rs 1,50,000. While in India, he has been working for a similar cement Company for a gross monthly remuneration equivalent to Nepalese Rs 1,00,000 per month. Determine the residential status and taxability of income of Mr. Ram Bansal in Nepal.

**Answer: 4(a)**

As per section 2(ka ang) of Income Tax Act, 2058, a natural person is a "resident" in an income year if -
a. His habitual place of adobe is Nepal
b. Has been staying in Nepal for the period of 183 days or more in a consecutive period of 365 days, or
c. Has been deputed in foreign service by Government of Nepal

So, considering the above provision of Income Tax Act, 2058 -
i) In the given case, Mr. Shyam Khadka has been in Nepal for a period of 183 days or more in an income year, he is resident of Nepal. Since, he is resident of Nepal, his income from USA is taxable in Nepal.

ii) In the given case, Ram Bansal has been in Nepal for a period of more than 183 days in Nepal in consecutive 365 days of an income year. Further, his major economic activity is also in Nepal in the concerned income year. So, he is resident of Nepal. And his global income will be taxable in Nepal. Accordingly, his Income from Nepal as well as income in India will be taxed in Nepal.

Note: Mr. Bansal seems to be working in India before joining the company in Nepal. So, by the residential criteria of India, he becomes a resident in India. He is also a resident of Nepal as per Income Tax Act of Nepal. In such situation, Article 4 of the DTAA between India and Nepal mentions that if an individual is a resident of both contracting States, where he has a permanent home available to him. In this way, Mr. Bansal becomes a resident of India. Thus, this question becomes out of syllabus.

### Question no. 8c (IT & VAT)
8. Write short notes:


c) Reverse Charge


**Answer:**
 c) Reverse Charge

Generally, VAT is collected and paid by the seller of goods. However, in some cases Act has cast responsibility upon the purchaser of goods to pay VAT. This responsibility of paying VAT on purchaser is termed as 'Reverse VAT'. Section 8(2) and 8(3) of Value Added Tax Act, 2052 has provided for below provisions for reverse VAT -
i. VAT for Service Received from Foreign Party:

As per section 8(2) of Value Added Tax Act, 2052, any person whether registered or not in Nepal receiving services from a person who is not registered and is outside Nepal shall have to assess and collect tax at the taxable value at the time of payment in accordance with this Act and the Rules framed under this Act.
ii. VAT from Housing Developers:

Similarly as per section 8(3) of Value Added Tax Act, 2052, If a construction of commercial purpose building or apartment or shopping complex or similar other structure as specified by the Department, of which value is more than Five Million Rupees, has been made from a person who is not registered, it shall be deemed as it has been constructed from registered person and shall deposit the tax. In case tax is not deposited, it shall be assessed and collected from the owner of such structure.
