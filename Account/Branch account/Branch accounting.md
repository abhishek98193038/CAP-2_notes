
# Branch accounting 



![[1788433601811.png]]


**Introduction** 
A branch can be described as an establishment carrying on either the same or substantially the same activities as carried on by head office of the company 

For accounting aspects branch maybe classified as:
1. Inland Branch 
	1. Dependent branch → for which whole accounting records are kept at head office 
	2. Independent branch → which maintains independent accounting records
2. Foreign branch 

## Dependent branch 
When the businesses policies and the administration of a branch are fully controlled by the head office and its accounts are also maintained by head office such branch is called dependent branch goods are transferred by head office to branch at 
1. Cost 
2. Selling price 
3. Cost + profit percentage

### Accounting for dependent branch
Dependent branch does not maintain a complete record of its transactions. The head office may maintain accounts of dependent branch in any of the following methods 
1. Debtor method 
2. Stock and debtor method 
3. Trading and profit and loss account method (final account method)

#### Debtor method 
Under this method the head office opens only one account for one branch called "Branch account" to compute profit or loss of the branch

#### Stock and Debtor method
Under this method the following accounts for a branch are maintained by the head office 
1. Branch stock account (similar to Branch trading account)
2. Branch debtor account 
3. Branch expense account 
4. Branch cash and bank account 
5. Branch fixed assets account 
6. Branch P/L account 
7. Branch creditor account

Notes: 
1. Credit the branch stock account with the value of closing stock at cost. Balancing figure of branch stock account will represent gross profit or gross loss 
2. Transfer gross profit or gross loss to branch profit and loss account. 
3. Transfer balance of branch expense account to the debit of branch P/L account
Branch profit and loss from Branch P/L account will be transferred to head office P/L account 
#### Final account method / trading and P/L account method

Under this method trading and P/L account are prepared separately for each branch as a separate entity

## Accounting for Independent branch

Following other features of accounting system of an independent branch 
1. Branch maintains its entire book of account under double entry system 
2. Branch opens a head office account in its books to record all the transactions entered between head office and branch. The head office maintains branch account to record these transactions 
3. Branch prepares its trial balance, trading and P/L account at the end of accounting period and sends copies of these statement to head office for incorporation 
4. After receiving the financial statement from branch . The head office reconciles between the two branch account in head office books and head office account in branch books 
5. The head office passes necessary journal entries to incorporate branch trial balance in its books

## Accounting for foreign branch

Generally foreign branch maintenance Independent and complete records of business transaction. Balances of foreign branch are incorporated in head office books by using applicable foreign exchange rate.
Foreign exchange rates used for transaction for foreign branch balance is given below 

1. Sir monitory items 
	1. Example : cash and bank balance, debtors , bills receivable, creditors, bills payables, loans etc 
	2. Excluding : Fixed assets ,inventory 
	3. Use closing exchange rate 
2. For inventory 
	1. Opening inventory → opening exchange rate 
	2. Closing inventory → closing exchange rate 
3. For non monetary asset ( Fixed assets)
	1. If fix assets are measured at cost then use exchange rate on the date of purchase 
	2. If fixed assets are measured at fair value then use exchange rate on the date of fair value measurement 
	3. Note: exchange rate applicable for depreciation of fixed assets is same as exchange rate for fixed asset 
4. For revenue items (income and expenses)
	1. Use average exchange rate , but if the rate on the date of transaction given in question then use that exchange rate 
	2.  $\text{average exchange rate = } \frac{\text{opening exchange rate + closing exchange rate}}{2}$
	3. Note: different figure of translation is transferred to foreign exchange translation reserve

When one branch does transaction with another branch head office also records such transactions in its books in case of dependent branch
