Banking Companies can be defined as banking and financial institution regulated by NRB for the purpose of accepting deposits from public and mobilization of such deposit among public or any other companies or enterprises . Banking and financial institution may also involve in any financial and non-financial business

<U> Minimum required capital</u>
Every license BFI , shall maintain minimum authorized, issued and paid up capital as may be prescribed by NRB in its directive from time to time . As per directive 2079; minimum paid up capital of BFI, shall be as below:

| C     | 80 cr          | 50 cr                                    |
| D     | 10 cr          | 2 cr                                     |

<U> General reserve fund </u>
As per the provision of sec 44 of BAFIA 2073, Every licensed banking or financial institution shall maintain reserve fund where in at least <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">20%</mark> ==of annual net profit== of such BFI shall be ==transfer till such fund becomes double of paid up capital== and ==then at least== <mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;">10%</mark> of net profit shall be transfered to such fund . General reserve fund shall not be utilized anywhere without permission of NRB.

<U> Exchange equalization fund </u>
As per provision of sec 45 of BAFIA 2073, Every ==BFIs which deals in foreign exchange business== shall maintain exchange equalization fund where in at least <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">25%</mark> ==of revaluation profit earned as a result of change in exchange rate== of foreign currency ==other than Indian currency== shall be ==transfer.== Such equalization fund shall not be utilized anywhere other than prescribed by NRB.

As per provision of directive 2079, every BFI shall maintain adequate and sound capital . Here, adequacy means quantity of capital and sound means quality of capital which all licensed BFIs shall have to maintain. Capital adequacy norms indicates the solvency or insolvency position of BFIs

<U>capital fund ratio </u>
Capital fund ratio can be calculated by following formula

1. Primary capital ratio (Tire I capital ratio)
$$
= \frac{\text{Primary Capital Fund}}{\text{Total Risk weighted exposure}} 
$$
2. Secondary capital ratio (Tire II capital ration)
$$
= \frac{\text{Secondary Capital Fund}}{\text{Total Risk weighted exposure}} 
$$
3. Total capital ratio
$$
= \frac {\text{Total capital}}{\text{Total risk weighted exposure}}
$$
$$
\text{Total capital = trer I capital + tier II capital}
$$
4. Total risk weighted exposure
	$$ 
	= \text{credit risk weighted exposure + Market risk weighted exposure + operational risk weighted exposure + supervisory adjustment}
	$$

<u>2. Basel II</u>

<U>A. Operational risk RWE </u>
Operational risk is the risk of loss arising due to inadequate internal control mechanism in terms of process, system and people from external events.
The most important type of operational risk involve breakdown in internal control and corporate governance which leads to financial loss through errors fraud, or failure to perform the work in timely manner by its dealer, lending officer or other staff. Operational risk also include major failure of information technology systems or events such as fire or other disaster.
Therefore operational risk RWE is calculated on operating income on the basis of past 3 years where risk weight is 15% prescribed by the NRB and then Total risk weighted exposure shall be multiplied by 9.09 times. Operational risk RWE shall be calculated as below:

<u>Liquidity ratio</u>
A. Cash reserve ratio (CRR) => includes cash

$$
CRR = \frac{\text{weekly average balance held with NRB}}{\text{ weekly average total deposit liabilities}} \times 100 

$$
As per NRB CRR must be $\geq$ 3%
where,
$$
\text{Weekly average} = \frac{\text{balance/deposit from sunday to saturday}}{7}
$$
During examination
$$
\text{ weekly average balance held with NRB on week 3 should be} = \frac{\text{CRR of week 2} + \text{CRR of week 1}}{2} \times \text{ weekly average total deposit liability of week 3 }
$$
$$
\text{ average CRR of previous 2 week} = \frac{\text{CRR of week 2} + \text{CRR of week 1}}{2}
$$

why? => new deposits should have 1 week time for sending 3% to NRB

A. Performing loan :
Performing loan can be classified in following categories
1. **Pass loan (good loan)**: 
	- Loans/advances which have not overdue and which are overdue by a period one month 
	- Loan against fixed deposit 
	- Loan against government securities and securities issued by Nepal Rastra Bank 
	- Gold loan 
	
2. **Watch List**:
	- Loans/advances which are overdue by a period from one month up to three months 
	- Short term loans which are not renewed within 1 month or such loans for which temporary extension of up to 90 days has been done
	-  borrowers which are in loss or having negative net-worth for consecutive period of 3 year or more. But this provision shall not be applicable in case of projects which are under construction phase 
	- if loans of more than 2 billion have not been converted into consortium loan 
	- loans provided to borrowers with debt-equity ratio of more than 80:20
	- loans which are provided by violating norms of debt-burden ratio (income to EMI ratio) as stated by NRB
	

B. Non-performing loan 

1. **Sub-standard**: Loans/advances which are overdue by a period from three months to a maximum period of six moths. 
2. **Doubtful**: Loans/advances which are overdue by a period from six-months to a maximum period of one year. 
3. **Bad or Loss loan**: 
	- Loans/advances which are overdue by a period of more than one year.
	- if borrower is declared insolvent 
	- if borrower is out of contact for a period of more than 90 days 
	- if loan is used for any other purpose or if it is mis-utilized
	- if project for which loan has been provided is not in operation 
	- forced loans which are overdue for more than 90 days 
	- loans for which court case relating to recovery is running or for which auction process has started
	- additional loans provided to blacklisted borrowers 
	- if credit card loans are overdue for a period of more than 90 days 
	- if borrowers have provided different financial report for the same period

**Discounting of Bills**
Every BFIs may involve in various types of any other financial or non-financial business. Out of which , bill discounting is the normal business transaction of BFI.
Discount income on bill discounted is credited whenever bills receivable is discounted but such income shall be recognized by dividing into two periods where income on bill discounted till the end of financial year occured shall be recognized as income and bill discounted income of unexpired period which is till the date of maturity in next financial year shall be recognized as income in respective next financial year
Bill discounted income for unexpired period shall be credited to rebate on bills discounted at the end of the financial year and it shall be credited to either directly in p&l a/c or bil discounted income in next financial year.

**Provision relating to cash reserve ratio (CRR)**
As per provisions of directive, 2079 of NRB, every licensed BFI shall maintain necessary cash reserve ration (CRR). As per such directive every BFIs having the license of 'A' class , 'B' class and 'C' class shall have to maintain CRR @ of ==4% of total deposited liabilities==
'D' class microfinancing institution shall have to maintain ==2% of its deposited liabilities== as CRR
**note**
1. At least 70% of specified CRR shall have to be deposited at NRB on daily basis.
2. If 'B' class, 'C' class and 'D' class of BFIs are operating in such a location where there is no office of NRB, then they have to maintain separate current account in 'A' class bank nearby their location where they have to maintain such CRR as NRB deposit
3. Deposit liabilities includes cash deposited by customer in current a/c, fixed deposit a/c and saving a/c

**Provision related to statutory liquidity ratio (SLR)**
As per provision of directive 2079, every BFIs shall have to maintain statutory liquidity ratio @ prescribed percentage of total domestic deposit as below
'A' class = 12% of domestic deposit
'B' class = 10% of domestic deposit
'C' class = 7% of domestic deposit
'D' class = 4% of domestic deposit
**Notes**
1. Liquid asset include the following cash balance of bank
	1. Cash balance of bank at their vault
	2. Investment in Nepal Government Securities
	3. Amount deposited at NRB for CRR purpose
	4. Amount deposited by 'B', 'C', and 'D' class bank in current a/c at 'A' class bank for CRR purpose
	5. Amount invested in international securities issued in Nepalese currency.
